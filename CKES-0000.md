# Canonical Knowledge Engineering System (CKES)

## Project Introduction

## 1. Purpose

The **Canonical Knowledge Engineering System (CKES)** is an independent architectural research, experimentation, and prototyping project concerned with determining how systems can acquire, organize, evaluate, canonicalize, retrieve, govern, evolve, and maintain canonical knowledge at practical scale.

CKES exists to investigate how the principles established by the **Canonical Representation Architecture (CRA)** can be realized operationally without making CRA dependent upon a particular:

- artificial intelligence model;
- database technology;
- vector database;
- embedding model;
- knowledge graph implementation;
- programming language;
- application domain;
- or source-data representation.

CKES is not itself the Canonical Representation Architecture.

CRA defines technology-independent architectural principles and requirements.

CKES provides an experimental environment in which candidate mechanisms for realizing those principles can be implemented, measured, challenged, and refined.

Findings from CKES may subsequently inform formal CRA specifications.

---

# 2. Project Relationships

CKES is an independent project with explicit relationships to several existing systems.

## 2.1 Engineering Documentation Framework (EDF)

CKES SHALL adopt the **Engineering Documentation Framework (EDF)**.

EDF governs how the CKES repository is:

- bootstrapped;
- structured;
- documented;
- evolved;
- reviewed;
- and maintained.

CKES documentation and engineering decisions should follow applicable EDF requirements.

EDF governs the engineering project.

It does not define CKES canonical knowledge semantics.

---

## 2.2 Canonical Representation Architecture (CRA)

CKES SHALL use the **Canonical Representation Architecture (CRA)** as its architectural foundation.

CRA provides the technology-independent architectural principles governing concepts including:

- canonical knowledge;
- canonical identity;
- canonical relationships;
- context;
- provenance;
- representations;
- evolution;
- and related architectural concerns.

CKES also serves as an experimental proving environment for unresolved CRA architectural questions.

The intended relationship is:

```text
CRA
 │
 │ architectural hypotheses,
 │ principles and requirements
 ▼
CKES
 │
 │ implement
 │ experiment
 │ measure
 │ challenge
 │ refine
 ▼
CKES Research Findings
 │
 ▼
CRA
 │
 ▼
Potential formal specifications
```

CKES MUST distinguish between:

- requirements already established by CRA;
- CKES implementation choices;
- experimental hypotheses;
- domain-specific policies;
- and findings that may eventually warrant formalization in CRA.

## 2.3 Culinary Arts Learning System (CALS)

The **Culinary Arts Learning System (CALS)** SHALL serve as the initial target knowledge domain for CKES experimentation.

Culinary knowledge provides a useful proving ground because it contains:

- broad concepts;
- highly specific concepts;
- processes;
- techniques;
- ingredients;
- equipment;
- recipes;
- procedural knowledge;
- contextual knowledge;
- regional variation;
- terminology differences;
- contradictory practices;
- subjective practices;
- safety-sensitive knowledge;
- and extensive natural-language source material.

CALS is CKES's initial experimental domain.

CALS MUST NOT become an architectural dependency of generic CKES canonicalization mechanisms.

---

## 2.4 The Recipe Vault

**The Recipe Vault** SHALL provide:

1. the initial source-data model for CKES/CALS experimentation;
2. an eventual real-world source of culinary knowledge contributions;
3. a practical test of incremental knowledge acquisition from an evolving operational system.

The existing Recipe Vault schema should be reused where practical.

However, CKES MUST NOT become tightly coupled to the Recipe Vault database implementation.

The long-term integration should occur through an explicit source adapter and change contract.

# 4. Central Research Problem

A CRA-adopting system may encounter enormous amounts of potentially useful information.

The architecture must determine:

- what information contains meaningful knowledge;
- what knowledge is already represented;
- what knowledge is genuinely novel;
- what should become canonical;
- what should merely support existing canonical knowledge;
- how knowledge should be decomposed;
- what deserves independent canonical identity;
- what concepts exist;
- what new concepts are justified;
- what contexts apply;
- what relationships exist;
- what evidence supports the knowledge;
- how conflicts are represented;
- and how canonical knowledge evolves.

Human curation cannot practically perform all of these operations at sufficient scale.

AI therefore appears necessary for substantial portions of semantic analysis.

However:

> **AI inference does not itself confer canonical authority.**

CKES must investigate how AI can perform most semantic work while canonical authority remains governed by explicit policy.

# 6. Canonical Concepts

A canonical concept provides stable semantic identity for something that needs to be independently recognized.

Initial CALS examples include:

## Subjects / Nouns

```text
Chicken
Beef
Pork
Seafood
Roux
Stock
Broth
Stew
Soup
Pastry
Sauce
Gravy
```

## Actions / Processes / Techniques

```text
Baking
Deep Frying
Pan Frying
Stir-Frying
Curing
Steaming
Marination
Dredging
```

A canonical concept is not necessarily itself a complete piece of usable canonical knowledge.

For example:

```text
Chicken
```

may possess canonical identity without itself constituting a detailed culinary knowledge object.

# 7. Canonical Relationships

Concepts and knowledge objects participate in semantic relationships.

Examples include:

```text
Fried Chicken
    --uses-technique-->
Deep Frying
```

```text
Popeye's Signature Fried Chicken
    --is-a-kind-of-->
Fried Chicken
```

```text
Popeye's Signature Fried Chicken
    --has-preparation-stage-->
Marination
```

```text
Velveting
    --commonly-precedes-->
Stir-Frying
```

Relationships must possess defined semantics and must ultimately conform to applicable CRA Canonical Relationships architecture.

CKES must not prematurely resolve architectural questions that remain under CRA discovery.

# 8. Semantic and Contextual Paths

CALS should be capable of constructing semantic paths such as:

```text

                -> Preparation
```

```text
Chicken
    -> Deep Frying
        -> Fried Chicken
            -> Popeye's Signature Fried Chicken
                -> Marination
```

```text

    -> Deep Frying
        -> Fried Chicken
            -> Popeye's Signature Fried Chicken
                -> Batter
```

```text
Chicken
    -> Deep Frying
        -> Fried Chicken
            -> Popeye's Signature Fried Chicken
                -> Frying
```

However:

> **A semantic, contextual, classification, or navigation path MUST NOT determine canonical identity.**

The same canonical knowledge may be reached through multiple paths.

Paths provide context and discoverability.

They do not define what the canonical knowledge fundamentally is.

# 9. Canonical Knowledge Objects

A canonical knowledge object represents a coherent, independently useful unit of knowledge warranting independent identity and lifecycle.

A working hypothesis is:

> **A canonical knowledge object should represent the smallest independently useful unit of knowledge that warrants its own identity, relationships, provenance, evolution, and lifecycle.**

This does not imply decomposition into individual sentences or assertions.

For example, this may be excessive:

```text
CK-1: Add salt.
CK-2: Add pepper.
CK-3: Stir.
CK-4: Refrigerate.
CK-5: Wait 12 hours.
```

A more useful canonical object might represent:

```text
Subject:
    Popeye's Signature Fried Chicken

Aspect:
    Marination

Knowledge:
    [coherent marination knowledge]

Ingredients:
    [...]

Conditions:
    [...]

Duration:
    [...]

Instructions:
    [...]
```

A key working principle is:

> **Structure does not imply independent canonical identity.**

---

# 10. Canonical Economy

CKES must investigate mechanisms that prevent uncontrolled proliferation of canonical identities.

The objective is not to canonicalize every statement encountered.

A working principle is:

> **Canonical representation should preserve independently meaningful and reusable knowledge without unnecessarily multiplying canonical identities.**

Incoming information may:

- map to existing concepts;
- reinforce existing knowledge;
- add evidence;
- establish provenance;
- strengthen relationships;
- create new relationships;
- extend existing knowledge;
- remain indexed source material;
- remain deferred;
- or have no canonical significance.

Processing 10,000 source items may legitimately produce zero new canonical objects.

# 12. Canonicalization Authority

Canonical status is conferred through governance.

Authority may derive from:

- a human owner;
- an organization;
- a repository authority;
- a standards organization;
- a governance process;
- or another explicitly recognized authority.

That authority may delegate routine canonicalization operations to automated mechanisms.

The automation derives authority from policy.

The AI does not derive authority from its own confidence.

# 14. Example Concept-Creation Policy

A new canonical concept should generally:

- represent a meaningful semantic distinction;
- not duplicate an existing canonical concept;
- possess meaning independent of source representation;
- have adequate provenance;
- integrate meaningfully with existing semantic structures;
- and improve reasoning, retrieval, representation, or knowledge organization.

A concept should not be created merely because:

- wording differs;
- a synonym was encountered;
- a different source uses another term;
- a different navigation path exists;
- or AI finds a new label convenient.

The system should prefer reuse of existing concepts over unnecessary concept creation.

# 15. Incremental and Demand-Driven Canonicalization

CKES should not assume that all available information must be continuously canonicalized.

Potential triggers include:

- newly acquired information;
- changed information;
- identified knowledge gaps;
- user queries exposing insufficient knowledge;
- new authoritative sources;
- contradictions;
- explicit research requests;
- and scheduled analysis of accumulated changes.

A working principle is:

> **The existence or availability of information does not itself require canonicalization.**

Knowledge should be allowed to grow organically.

# 21. Synthetic Dataset Strategy

The current Recipe Vault dataset is not sufficiently large or diverse for meaningful CKES experimentation.

CKES should therefore generate a controlled synthetic Recipe Vault-compatible corpus.

The objective is not simply to generate a large number of recipes.

The corpus must provide known experimental conditions.

Synthetic recipes should be generated according to a controlled **Synthetic Corpus Manifest** or equivalent experiment definition.

---

# 22. Initial Corpus Sizes

CKES should grow the synthetic corpus progressively.

The initial recommended stages are:

| Stage | Approximate Recipes | Primary Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Seed | 50 | Debug schema, pipeline, extraction, concepts and relationships |
| POC-1 | 500 | First meaningful semantic novelty and duplication experiments |
| POC-2 | 5,000 | Hybrid/vector retrieval, canonical economy and cost |
| POC-3 | 25,000 | Scalability and knowledgebase-growth analysis |
| Stress | 100,000+ | Large-scale testing only after earlier architecture succeeds |

These are experimental starting points rather than fixed architectural requirements.

Cursor should refine them if implementation evidence suggests better thresholds.

The project should NOT begin by generating hundreds of thousands of recipes.

Small controlled datasets are necessary for understanding failures.

# 31. Semantic Novelty

A candidate must not be admitted as independently identified canonical knowledge until a reasonable attempt has been made to determine whether equivalent or related canonical knowledge already exists.

For example:

```text
A coating technique in which food passes through
wet and dry coating stages more than once.
```

may already be represented by:

```text
Repeat the flour-buttermilk-flour dredging sequence
to build a thicker crust.
```

String comparison alone is insufficient.

AI semantic reasoning will be required for some cases.

However, AI should not search the complete knowledgebase directly.

---

# 32. Hybrid Semantic Retrieval

CKES should investigate:

```text
Exact Identity Matching
        +
Normalized Matching
        +
Structured Concept Matching
        +
Relationship/Context Matching
        +
Full-Text Retrieval
        +
Vector Similarity
        ↓
Candidate Existing Knowledge
        ↓
AI Semantic Adjudication
```

Cheap retrieval mechanisms should narrow the candidate set before expensive AI semantic reasoning occurs.

---

# 33. Vector Analysis

CKES should investigate vector embeddings for semantic candidate retrieval.

Vector search should answer:

> **What existing knowledge is semantically close enough to this candidate that it should be examined?**

Vector search does NOT answer:

> **Is this the same canonical knowledge?**

That requires semantic reasoning and canonicalization policy.

---

# 34. Vectors Are Derived Representations

Vectors are not canonical knowledge.

They are derived retrieval representations.

```text
CANONICAL KNOWLEDGE
       │
       ├── Graph Representation
       ├── Search Representation
       ├── Relational Representation
       └── Vector Representation
```

Changing the embedding model may require rebuilding the vector index without changing canonical knowledge.

Embedding metadata should include sufficient information to identify the model and representation used.

---

# 35. AI Semantic Adjudication

After hybrid retrieval identifies likely matches, AI should receive a bounded comparison problem.

AI should classify relationships such as:

```text
EQUIVALENT

SUBSUMED_BY_EXISTING

MORE_SPECIFIC_THAN_EXISTING

MORE_GENERAL_THAN_EXISTING

PARTIALLY_OVERLAPS

EXTENDS_EXISTING

CONTRADICTS

DISTINCT

UNCERTAIN
```

This bounded comparison is intended to reduce:

- token consumption;
- AI cost;
- hallucination opportunities;
- and scalability problems.

---

# 36. Canonicalization Actions

Semantic classifications may lead to:

```text
EQUIVALENT
    -> reuse existing canonical identity
    -> optionally associate additional evidence

SUBSUMED_BY_EXISTING
    -> normally avoid new identity

EXTENDS_EXISTING
    -> propose extension or related knowledge

CONTRADICTS
    -> preserve conflict
    -> never silently overwrite

DISTINCT
    -> evaluate new canonical identity

UNCERTAIN
    -> defer, investigate, or escalate
```

Canonicalization policy governs the actual permitted action.

---

# 40. Canonical Knowledge Maintenance

CKES must investigate maintenance as well as acquisition.

Existing canonical knowledge may later be discovered to:

- duplicate other knowledge;
- overlap unnecessarily;
- contradict other knowledge;
- require merging;
- require splitting;
- contain obsolete relationships;
- require supersession;
- or require retirement.

Maintenance changes must pass through the same governance mechanisms as acquisition changes.

---

# 41. Human Governance at Scale

Humans cannot individually verify every canonical object in a large knowledgebase.

Human governance should therefore focus primarily on the process.

Humans may:

- establish policy;
- establish authority;
- approve delegated automated authority;
- review policy changes;
- inspect anomalies;
- review high-risk knowledge;
- review unresolved conflicts;
- audit samples;
- measure canonicalization quality;
- and intervene when automation fails.

---

# 42. Risk-Based Canonicalization

Knowledge risk should affect canonicalization requirements.

Example CALS categories might include:

## Lower Risk

- terminology;
- history;
- cuisine classification;
- stylistic practices.

## Ordinary Culinary Knowledge

- recipes;
- techniques;
- ingredient behavior;
- cooking processes.

## Higher Risk

- food safety;
- allergens;
- toxicity;
- preservation safety;
- hazardous preparation practices.

Higher-risk knowledge should require stronger evidence and stricter policy.

Synthetic POC information must never be treated as authoritative evidence for real-world high-risk claims.

# 43. AI Cost Control

A central CKES research objective is determining whether AI-driven canonicalization is economically practical.

AI usage should be minimized through:

- change-based processing;
- deduplication;
- hashing;
- normalization;
- structured lookup;
- full-text retrieval;
- vector retrieval;
- semantic caching;
- persisted semantic representations;
- batching;
- bounded AI comparisons;
- reuse of prior analysis;
- inexpensive models for routine work;
- stronger models only when necessary;
- and demand-driven processing.

CKES must measure AI cost rather than merely demonstrate technical feasibility.

# 45. Canonical Growth Hypothesis

CKES should explicitly test the hypothesis that canonical knowledge growth should become progressively slower than source-data growth as the knowledgebase matures.

For example, the system might observe behavior conceptually resembling:

```text
First 500 recipes:

    many new concepts
    many new knowledge objects

Next 5,000 recipes:

    fewer new concepts per recipe
    fewer new knowledge objects per recipe
    more mappings to existing knowledge

Next 25,000 recipes:

    still fewer new concepts per recipe
    still fewer new CK objects per recipe
    increasing evidence-only outcomes
```

The specific numbers are unknown and must be measured.

The desired property is:

```text
Source Volume
     ↑↑↑

Canonical Novelty Rate
     ↓
```

If concepts and canonical knowledge objects continue to grow approximately linearly with recipe count, CKES must investigate whether:

- canonicalization policy is too permissive;
- knowledge boundaries are too granular;
- semantic equivalence detection is failing;
- concept reuse is failing;
- or the underlying architectural hypothesis is incorrect.

---

# 46. AI Cost Scaling Hypothesis

CKES should also test whether AI cost per source item declines as semantic knowledge matures.

The hypothesis is:

```text
Early Knowledgebase

Large proportion of information is novel
        ↓
More AI reasoning
        ↓
Higher cost per source item
```

followed by:

```text
Mature Knowledgebase

Large proportion maps to known concepts
and known knowledge
        ↓
Cheap retrieval resolves more cases
        ↓
AI handles fewer ambiguous cases
        ↓
Lower cost per source item
```

This is not assumed to be true.

It must be experimentally measured.

Failure of AI cost to flatten is an important architectural finding.

    ↓
Add POC-2 Corpus
    ↓

    ↓

This allows CKES to measure whether increasing knowledge maturity affects:

- novelty;
- retrieval;
- canonical growth;
- AI usage;
- AI cost;
- and processing time.

Separate reset-based experiments may still be useful for reproducibility and comparison.

# 49. Initial CALS Research Program

The first CKES research program should include:

## Experiment A — Semantic Duplicate Detection

Can differently worded knowledge be reliably mapped to existing canonical knowledge?

## Experiment B — Novel Knowledge Detection

Can genuinely novel knowledge be distinguished from paraphrases and minor variants?

## Experiment C — Concept Discovery

Can AI determine when a new concept is warranted?

## Experiment D — Relationship Discovery

Can useful semantic relationships be proposed without graph inflation?

## Experiment E — Knowledge Boundary Determination

Can AI consistently produce coherent reusable knowledge objects without excessive fragmentation?

## Experiment F — Vector Retrieval

Compare:

- lexical retrieval;
- structured retrieval;
- vector retrieval;
- hybrid retrieval.

## Experiment G — Semantic Adjudication

Evaluate classifications such as:

```text
EQUIVALENT
SUBSUMED
EXTENDS
CONTRADICTS
DISTINCT
UNCERTAIN
```

## Experiment H — Canonical Economy

Determine what proportion of source information actually warrants new canonical identity.

## Experiment I — AI Cost

Measure:

- calls;
- tokens;
- monetary cost;
- processing time;
- and cost per accepted canonical contribution.

## Experiment J — Scale

Increase corpus size progressively and identify bottlenecks.

## Experiment K — Maintenance

Seed duplicates, overlaps, and conflicts and test whether CKES detects them.

## Experiment L — Source Revision

Modify previously processed recipes and determine whether CKES correctly limits reprocessing to affected knowledge.

## Experiment M — Source Deletion

Remove evidence sources and determine what happens to canonical knowledge previously supported by those sources.

## Experiment N — Knowledge Maturity

Determine whether novelty rate declines as the canonical knowledgebase grows.

## Experiment O — Cost Maturity

Determine whether AI cost per source item declines as semantic structure and canonical knowledge mature.

# 58. Initial Project Objective

The first major CKES objective is:

> **Build a reproducible CALS-targeted proof of concept using an isolated Recipe Vault-compatible source database, populate it with a deliberately controlled synthetic culinary corpus, simulate the incremental lifecycle of a growing Recipe Vault, and experimentally determine whether hybrid retrieval, AI semantic reasoning, explicit canonicalization policy, controlled canonical commits, and canonical economy can produce and maintain useful canonical knowledge at practical cost and scale.**

The POC must be designed so that the synthetic Recipe Vault-compatible source can later be replaced by the actual Recipe Vault without redesigning generic CKES canonicalization mechanisms.

Success is not defined as creating the largest possible culinary knowledgebase.

Success is defined as learning whether the architecture can reliably and economically determine:

> **What new or changed source information actually deserves to change what the system canonically knows?**
